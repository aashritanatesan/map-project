# Book 1 chapter summaries

## Chapter 1
Cass Reed comes up the fourteen locks to Stonebridge by barge and sees a hunter's cart carrying a dead, half-shifted wolf from the lapsed east spur of Highwood ground (400 acres, lapsed last spring under a separate line). At the Survey Office she walks the slate floor map in her socks and meets Registrar Voss, whose hands are oddly ink-free. He gives her the Highwood line (31 cairns), a deadline of first snow (about six weeks away), and a Clause Nine reminder, which Cass jokes about. She notices the line between Cairns Nine and Fourteen has been recut a quarter mile inward; Voss blames the ravine. At the inn she finds page 41 of June's notes missing. End: curious, uneasy, determined.

## Chapter 2
The innkeeper predicts snow in "three weeks or tomorrow". Climbing out of the city, Cass passes the Mining Guild's quarries and Guild stakes on the lapsed east spur, which the Guild bought from the Office. At the Highwood gate she meets Mara, who won't shake her hand and reads Voss's letter: if Cass is harmed, the line is "unwalked". Mara says June "was good" and hints at a wound. She admits nobody else would escort Cass. The stream tastes of blood. End: wary, stung, a little warmer.

## Chapter 3
Day one on the line. Cass realises she needs Mara to hold the brass chain handle, and Mara agrees. Cass crosses the scree above the ravine despite her terror. A careless flick of the chain burns Mara's right wrist with silver; Cass is horrified and gives her salve, and their fingertips touch. At Cairn Three, Mara smells River scouts and they move camp at night. End: guilt, attraction, alarm.

## Chapter 4
A night march by smell to a hollow. By the fire: venison, banter, and Mara deflecting "can you change?" Her burn isn't healing; Highwood has lost miles of ground survey by survey. At dawn they watch six Highwood wolves carry Aled's body across the open east spur in human form and shift the moment they cross the border cairn. Cass understands that her chain decides where they can be wolves. "Now you know what you're carrying." End: shaken, awed, responsible.

## Chapter 5
They chain Cairns Four to Six. At lunch, Cass says numbers are a floor to stand on; Mara says Ruth sings border songs. Cairn Seven is freshly painted with River's mark. Theo of River offers to take over the escort, and Cass refuses. He needles Mara: he last saw June walking with her toward Cairn Eight. Leaving, he says Voss asked him to watch Cass: "When you get to Nine, measure twice." End: suspicion, worry for Mara.

## Chapter 6
Mara won't go near Cairn Eight. Cass realises the line in each surveyor's mark points to the next cairn. The Office's Cairn Nine is a new, neat cairn built this summer, and Cass's triangle doesn't close. On the old bearing, a quarter mile out, she finds the real Nine toppled under a fallen larch, with a pry-bar gouge and no slip anywhere. Afraid of being wrong or starting a war, she tells Mara it "probably" fell in the slip. She keeps two sets of numbers. Mara: "You count when you lie, too." A storm rolls in. End: guilt, fear, resolve.

## Chapter 7
The storm drives them to the goat herders' bothy. They change back to back and share one tin cup of tea. Mara says Ruth is dying and that she'll inherit a shrinking pack. Cass tells her about the bribed surveyor who cost her family their farm: "the surveyor nobody could pay". Mara asks about Nine; Cass says "not sure yet", and Mara says to tell her when she's sure. In the dark, Mara confesses that she left June at Eight when June sent her away. They hold hands. At dawn Mara smells pitch smoke: hunters. End: closeness, dread.

## Chapter 8
The line from Nine to Ten crosses a neck of open ground. Mara steps onto it and dims. Three hunters ambush them, and the leader says "Office told us there'd be no trouble on this stretch", with Guild bounties doubled for Highwood. Cass steps in front of Mara, cites the treaty and whips the chain around a hunter's arm; Mara draws on the leader, and the hunters retreat. Back on Highwood ground Cass collapses. Mara, furious and frightened: "Don't ever do that again." She wipes mud from Cass's cheek. Tomorrow: the ravine. End: adrenaline crash, tenderness.

## Chapter 9
Cairn Ten is easy, and the ravine lies between it and Eleven. They climb down. Mara shows Cass the seven-stone memorial she built for June. Cass notices the rockfall looks staged: fresh stones under mossy ones. A figure moves on the rim, and a second rockfall comes down. Mara tackles Cass to safety; a shard cuts Cass's palm and Mara's forearm, and their blood mixes. Cass feels a second heartbeat, Mara's pain, her direction and her scent. Mara: "No. Not you. Not now." End: shock, wonder, fear.

## Chapter 10
Shared pain is undeniable; Mara flinches when Cass's wound is touched. Cass can sense Mara's location with her eyes shut. Climbing to Eleven, Mara finds the pry bar on the rim, scented of lamp oil and paper. June's mark on Eleven has a tiny "13" beside it. Mara camps thirty yards away; Cass is hurt by her distance. At night Cass follows Mara's grief to the rim and finds her crying. In the moonlight they both have gold rings in their eyes. "It's called the tether. And it's going to get one of us killed." End: dread, longing.

## Chapter 11
Mara explains the tether: rare, unbreakable, shared pain, the compass sense, the tell (it can't be hidden at full moon), and impossible for humans. Cass realises that Clause Nine's "bond" means them: death for Mara, Cass's chain hand, and the line voided. Mara admits "not you" meant not someone she could lose like this. They agree to hide it, keep things strictly business, and stay apart at the hold, where Ruth would recognise it. They shake on it and both feel it. Mara reveals June once asked whether a human could catch the tether. End: fear, tenderness, mystery.

## Chapter 12
Strictly business fails comically and tenderly: a shared stubbed toe, Cass writing down a wrong angle, Mara missing a hare, Mara catching Cass on a ledge. At Cairn Thirteen, following June's "13", Mara smells wax and June at a loose stone. Behind it, an oilcloth package sealed J.M.: June's field book, her duplicate of page 41, and a note: "To the next surveyor. If you've found this, they've already tried to kill you." End: chill, purpose.

## Chapter 13
June's note: the Office line is false (cairns moved inward in three sections, four miles of border a quarter mile deep). Trust the marks. File in front of witnesses. She sent Mara away to protect her. Mara breaks down. Cass walks her through June's flawless numbers, then confesses her lie at Nine and shows her own hidden working: "Now I'm sure." They lay out the evidence: Voss, Theo, the hunters, the pry bar. Plan: walk the true line and file it publicly. First real trust. End: resolve, warmth.

## Chapter 14
Arrival at the hidden Highwood hold: carved song-posts, a long fire, fifty watchful wolves. Lena, Mara's cousin, confronts Cass. Ruth, small and ailing but razor-sharp, reads June's book, smells the pry bar ("fear"), and leads the hall in singing the Long Wood. She pledges Highwood's support. Noticing the too-wide gap on the bench, she orders Cass shuttered in the guesthouse for the full moon in two nights: "Whatever the moon has to show me, I'd rather not see it in my hall." End: suspicion, nerves.

## Chapter 15
Bet shows Cass around the guesthouse, plays with the theodolite, and tells her Lena will be Mara's second. Cass draws the true line in the hall; Ruth warns her that believing a line can be true is dangerous. Mara keeps away, and Cass feels every draw of her bow. On full-moon day the hold is restless, and Lena warns Cass to stay barred in. At dusk Mara comes to check the bar, confesses the tether is the loudest thing in her head, and they kiss for the first time. Mara runs as the moon rises and turns; the pack sings. Cass doesn't close the door. End: longing, awe.

## Chapter 16
Cass steps out into the full moon and watches the pack run and play. Through the tether she feels Mara's wolf joy: speed, strength, a thousand smells. Lena's dark wolf nearly attacks her; Mara's black wolf drives Lena off and comes to Cass, who holds her. A small white wolf, Ruth, sees the gold rings in both their eyes and walks away silently. Cass vows to make the line right for every wolf. At dawn Bet summons her to Ruth, alone. End: dread, resolve.

## Chapter 17
The pack sleeps off the moon. Ruth confronts Cass, who admits the tether. Ruth tells of her tethered parents. She cares about her daughter more than the forest: finish the line, file it, keep Mara out of Stonebridge at full moon, then go home. Cass pushes back: sending someone away isn't keeping them safe. Ruth: finish the line first, then "who gets a say". Lena will remember. Ruth gives Cass June's letter: "Don't look for me under the stones ... the songs are wrong about one thing." The Office is sending a second surveyor to Fifteen with Theo. End: shaken, suspicious.

## Chapter 18
Cass and Mara return to the line, strained. Mara secretly gives Cass her blanket. Above the tree line, Cass admits Ruth told her to go home; Mara: "Nobody ever asks what I want." Lena's team is rebuilding Nine and Twelve. At Fifteen: Theo, and Ambrose Holt of the Office, ink-fingered and smelling of the same lamp oil, insisting on the Office line. Theo passes on Voss's sympathy for the "accident at the ravine", which nobody reported. "He knows because he sent it." End: chill.

## Chapter 19
Cass's morning count: 99 links. Holt removed one while "admiring" the chain, to discredit her distances. She repairs it and confronts him before Theo. Cairns 16 to 18 match. Cass and Mara argue about Nineteen: Mara wants to hide the true line from Holt, but Cass insists a survey isn't a negotiation and she'll chain the truth in front of anyone. Mara concedes. At dawn Holt is gone with Cass's field book and Office copy, and Mara has gone after him into the Hollow, which is open ground. End: alarm.

## Chapter 20
Theo offers to help; Cass sends him to tell Ruth. Cass crosses the Hollow alone, feels Mara beaten and bound, and crosses a sinkhole bridge on hands and knees, counting chain links. She finds the hunters' camp: Mara tied to a post, her bow string cut, the field book on a stone, and the leader holding a silver knife and talking of taking a hand or an ear. Mara's look says "run". Cass doesn't. End: terror, resolve.

## Chapter 21
Cass levers a boulder into the hollow and the pony stampedes through the camp. She chain-whips the knife free and cuts Mara loose; Mara stabs a hunter with an arrow; Cass grabs her field book. They flee through the steam to a hidden hot-spring cave. Mara: "Why didn't you run?" Cass: "Because it was you." They make love in the pool, the tether doubling everything. Cass: "I'm not going back to the lowlands." Mara: "Nobody's sending anybody away." End: joy, peace.

## Chapter 22
Morning in the cave: a shared oatcake, tender teasing, Cass rewrapping Mara's wrists. Holt has read all of Cass's private notes. They face the cost and neither regrets it. Cass tells Mara the full exchange with Ruth. Snow is coming: three or four days on the peaks. They commit to finishing. Outside, Hollow people with spears appear; their elder smells the bond: "Two in one lifetime." End: surprise, hope.

## Chapter 23
The Hollow's underground town and its permanently half-shifted people. The elder returns the pry bar and Mara's bow, reports Holt walking to the city, and reveals Highwood once exiled her kin under Clause Twelve. Mara is ashamed: "Nobody carved a song for the ones we threw out." In a moonbeam, the elder sees Cass's gold ring and admits she once saw a human catch the tether, but won't name who. At parting she gives Cass a brass chain link scratched "J.M." End: wonder, purpose.

## Chapter 24
The Hollow boy leads them above Twenty. The oath card defines first snow as snow recorded in Stonebridge, which buys time. Lena and fourteen Highwood arrive: Theo kept his word. They rebuild the true cairns, singing, and Cass chains 20 to 23 and 27. At Twenty-Nine, River's council sings River's song on the Highwood cairn to provoke war. Mara restrains Highwood, and Cass chains straight through the council. Theo signals "not my choice". The first flakes fall on the peaks. End: defiance.

## Chapter 25
Thirty-One is closed in the snow on the summit. Theo warns that Holt was sent "to look" at their eyes: Voss suspects the tether. They run south by torchlight. Mara adds an eighth stone to June's memorial, and Cass marks it to point north. True Twelve, then true Nine (57 chains), as snow falls in the forest. The line is complete, four miles restored. Lena sings the Long Wood; Mara kisses Cass in front of the pack. End: triumph.

## Chapter 26
Ruth is carried down in a litter with fifty Highwood as witnesses. Everyone takes off their boots for the map room. Voss and Holt accuse Cass of a short chain; it's measured against the bronze Standard and is exact. Theo testifies about the 99 links. Cass lays out both surveyors' books and the pry bar and files the true line. Voss sacrifices Holt and orders it carved. The clerks recut four miles of border; Ruth weeps; Mara takes Cass's hand. First snow falls on Stonebridge. The line has held. End: victory.

## Chapter 27
A celebration at the inn. Theo deflects Cass's question about "she" ("Ask me in the spring"). Ruth tells Cass she's listening to what Mara wants. In Cass's room, Mara, dimmed by the city, says it: Cass stays, Mara lives, Cass keeps her hand. Cass: Clause Nine is a line, and lines can be put back where they belong. Mara: "Then survey it ... I'll hold the chain." Cass promises. Ridge song at the end. End: love, determination.

## Chapter 28
First-snow morning: the innkeeper and the chestnut woman show quiet allegiance (the latter descended from an exiled Highwood woman). The nervous clerk brings a summons: Cass must answer a Clause Nine charge at noon, all her surveys including the Highwood line are suspended, and there's a warrant for Mara's arrest. The witness statement is signed by June Marsh, who walked in out of the snow last night. Mara reads it: "That's June's hand." Highwood wakes, knives out. "June Marsh is alive." END OF BOOK 1 (cliffhanger).
