# Market Research Brief: Trilogy 01

Run date: 2026-10-28 · Agent: 1. Market Research · Run type: full scan (new series)

**Inputs used**
- Category: sapphic romance (Amazon Kindle: Lesbian Romance)
- Trope of interest: werewolf fated mates (the spec's example). No other trope was given.
- Constraints: none given, so these come from the voice guide: a fantasy or paranormal world that is really dangerous, a newcomer narrator, slow burn, and one or two fully on-page love scenes.

## 1. Tropes selling now (ranked by how often they appear in chart snapshots and 2026 roundups)

1. **Age gap / forbidden** (boss, professor, best friend's mom). The top of the chart is full of these, e.g. *Breaking the Rules: A Steamy Sapphic Age Gap Forbidden Romance*.
2. **Bi-awakening / "straight girl"** (*Straight Girl in My Bed*, *Her Neighbor's Wife*).
3. **Sports, especially hockey** (Phoenix Ridge Hockey series, *Power Play, Pretty Girl*).
4. **Friends to lovers / forced proximity.**
5. **Billionaire / sugar / nanny** (*The Billionaire's Nanny*, *The Sugar Wife*).
6. **Slow burn with emotional healing.** Roundups call this a fast-growing segment.
7. **Paranormal / romantasy, mainly vampires.** It's rising, but most of the buzz is trad-published (*Where Shadows Meet*, *The Book of Blood and Roses*) and not KU indie.

Pattern: the indie top 20 is almost all Kindle Unlimited, roughly half of it is series, and the steamy contemporary books lead. Subtitles carry the trope stack ("A Steamy Sapphic ___ Romance").

## 2. Oversaturated

- **Contemporary age-gap / boss / professor:** many look-alike titles, subtitles and covers.
- **"Perfect for fans of *Gideon the Ninth*"** comps in sapphic fantasy, which reviewers now name as a cliché.
- **Sapphic vampires (academy / gothic)** are filling up quickly on the trad side in 2026. Indie books can still enter, but they'd be competing with publisher marketing.
- General romantasy is crowded, and agents and editors say they are less hungry for it than two years ago (this applies to trad more than KU).

## 3. Unmet reader requests (paraphrased themes, not review text)

- Readers say rec lists are dominated by M/M and ask where the sapphic version of the big M/F romantasy hits is.
- They want sapphic fantasy where the queerness is **casual and built into the world**, not the conflict.
- **Sapphic shifter / werewolf fated mates on KU is thin.** Searches turn up a handful of one-offs and fox shifters, and no recognised werewolf series. M/F fated mates is one of KU's biggest engines.
- Some readers want **darker, morally grey** love interests and villain-coded heroines (a trend across romantasy).
- They want series that **keep one couple** rather than switching to a new pair each book (a common complaint about indie lesfic series).

**The gap:** a steamy, binge-able, KU-priced sapphic paranormal **trilogy about one couple**, built on fated-mates beats that M/F readers already buy in bulk. The voice guide already fits it.

## 4. Three trilogy concepts

### A. *Moonbound* (werewolf fated mates) — **MY PICK**
- **Hook:** A human surveyor hired to redraw a disputed border discovers that the pack heir sent to stop her is her fated mate, and the treaty she's enforcing makes their bond a capital crime.
- **Trope stack:** fated mates, enemies to lovers, forbidden, forced proximity (wilderness survey), grumpy/sunshine, protective alpha heroine.
- **World:** mountain borderlands where three packs and a human city keep a fragile peace by mapping and policing every border. The bond is real magic with costs: shared pain, and scent that can't be hidden.
- **Arc across three books:** Book 1, the bond snaps and they hide it. Book 2, the treaty breaks and they're on opposite sides of a war. Book 3, they rewrite the law together and she claims a place in the pack.
- **Why it fits:** it's the clearest gap in the scan (fated mates is huge in M/F and nearly absent in sapphic KU), the newcomer narrator fits naturally, and the subtitle sells itself: "A Steamy Sapphic Werewolf Fated Mates Romance".
- **Reason for the pick:** it is where the demand-to-supply mismatch is clearest, and it matches the trope you named. Fated-mates readers also binge series, which suits a monthly trilogy.

### B. *A Contract in Red* (vampire marriage of convenience)
- **Hook:** To clear her family's blood debt, a bookbinder signs a one-year marriage contract with the vampire lady who owns the city's nights, and both of them break the no-feelings clause.
- **Trope stack:** marriage of convenience, morally grey love interest, age gap (immortal), slow burn, "touch her and die".
- **World:** a gaslit river city where vampire Houses legally own debt. The night markets and blood-tithe laws put pressure on the marriage.
- **Why it fits:** it uses rising vampire demand and the "morally grey" request, and it borrows age-gap heat from the contemporary chart. Risk: vampires are filling up fastest.

### C. *The Iron Tithe* (fae court bodyguard)
- **Hook:** A blacksmith's daughter whose touch burns fae is given to the Unseelie court as tribute, and the queen's coldest knight is ordered to keep her alive until the solstice.
- **Trope stack:** bodyguard, forced proximity, bargains, enemies to lovers, ice queen / sunshine, "who did this to you".
- **World:** a fae court where iron is both her curse and their weakness. Each book has a deadly court trial or season.
- **Why it fits:** fae bargains are a proven M/F engine and there are few sapphic KU equivalents. Risk: it's the closest of the three to well-known M/F comps, so the vibe must stay distinct with no borrowed names, courts or plot shapes.
