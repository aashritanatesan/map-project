# World document: *Moonbound* trilogy

Series Planner, Part 1 · Draft v1 · **awaiting your approval**

## The setting: the Reach

The Reach is a high country of grey slate ridges, larch forest and sinkholes, where a cold river drops out of the mountains to the lock city of **Stonebridge**. Nothing here is flat and nothing stays put. Rain cuts new ravines each spring, sinkholes open overnight, and rockfalls move streams. That's why the borders have to be walked again and again.

**Sensory anchors**
1. **Smell:** wet slate after rain, larch resin, woodsmoke trapped under fog, and the sulphur breath of the hollows where hot springs leak up.
2. **Sound:** the clink of the surveyor's chain dragged over stone. Wolf song carries for miles at moonrise, and every pack's song is different.
3. **Colour:** border cairns painted with red ochre, re-daubed after every survey. In autumn the larches turn gold and drop their needles in soft heaps.
4. **Taste:** spring water that tastes of iron and blood, which locals say is how you know you've crossed into Highwood land.
5. **Touch:** frost on the theodolite's brass, needle-litter that silences footsteps, and the ache of thin air above the tree line.
6. **Stonebridge itself:** a city of locks, sluices and ledger halls, stacked up a gorge. The Survey Office sits at the top, and its great map room has a slate floor with the whole Reach carved into it.

## Rules of the world

**Original to this world**
1. **The Seven-Year Chain.** Under the Cairn Treaty, a border counts only if a sworn human surveyor has walked and chained it within the last seven years. If the deadline passes, the land lapses into **open ground**: no pack holds it, and anyone may hunt there, wolves included, with no penalty. Maps here aren't records. They are the law.
2. **Shifting follows the map, not the moon.** A wolf can fully shift only on ground its pack holds by treaty. On open ground or another pack's land, a wolf is stuck in human form, or at most a painful half-shift. Every survey therefore decides who can turn into a wolf, and where.
3. **The full moon overrides it at home.** On their own ground at full moon, wolves can't stay human. Every full moon, each pack is locked into wolf form on its own land and can't negotiate, travel or hide.
4. **The surveyor's chain is silver-linked.** The 66-foot measuring chain has every tenth link in silver. It's an instrument, a badge of office and a weapon all at once. Wolves can't touch it bare-handed, so they can't tamper with a measurement.
5. **Sung borders.** A pack claims land by singing at its cairns. The Survey Office writes each pack's song down in its ledgers as legal evidence, so a song sung on the wrong cairn is a declaration of war.

**The bond (the "tether")**
6. A fated bond is rare. It catches through scent and blood together: one shared wound is enough. Once caught, it can't be broken except by death.
7. A bonded pair **share pain** and gain a **compass sense**: each always knows the direction of the other, which is a gift and a liability for a surveyor.
8. **The tell:** under moonlight, a bonded person's irises carry a thin gold ring. Indoors and in daylight it can be hidden. At full moon, it can't.
9. **Treaty Clause Nine:** no wolf may bond with, marry or bed a sworn surveyor, because a surveyor who is bonded to a pack can't be trusted to measure fairly. The penalty is death for the wolf, and for the surveyor the loss of her chain hand and every survey she has ever signed voided. Clause Nine has never been invoked, because no one believes a human can catch the tether.

## Social structure and factions

**The Survey Office of Stonebridge (humans)**
- Sworn surveyors are trained in the lowland capital, rotated through the Reach, and officially neutral.
- The **Registrar**, Voss, runs the office. He is courteous, ledger-obsessed and quietly corrupt: Stonebridge's mining guild wants border land to lapse so that it can buy open ground for slate and silver.
- The previous surveyor of the Highwood line, June, died on the route last spring. Her death was officially a rockfall. It wasn't.

**The three packs**
- **The Highwood pack** holds the high larch forests and the oldest cairns. It's proud, insular and matriarchal, and loses ground every survey cycle. Its alpha, Ruth, is ailing, so her daughter **Mara** is heir.
- **The River pack** holds the river valleys. It's mercantile and allied with Stonebridge, trades furs and safe passage for favourable surveys, and is charming and untrustworthy.
- **The Hollow** holds the sinkhole country and has no legal ground at all: exiles, the half-shifted, wolves cast out under old clauses. The Hollow has every reason to want the treaty torn up.

**Open-ground hunters:** bounty crews who follow lapsed borders, hunting wolves who are caught in human form on land where killing them is legal.

## The couple

- **Narrator: Cass Reed**, 27, a newly sworn surveyor from the lowlands, on her first posting in the Reach. She was sent because nobody senior would take June's route. She's warm, wry and over-prepared, terrified of heights, and very good with numbers. She talks to her instruments.
- **Love interest: Mara**, 30, Highwood heir. She's sent to escort Cass, officially to protect her and actually to make sure the survey doesn't lose Highwood another mile. She's grim, economical with words, devastating with a bow, and carries her pack's losses like a bruise.

## How the world pressures the romance

**What keeps them apart**
- **Clause Nine:** the bond is a capital crime for Mara and ruin for Cass, so every touch carries a death sentence.
- **Conflict of interest:** each chain-length Cass measures can take land, and with it the power to shift, from Mara's pack. Doing her job honestly could hurt the woman she loves.
- **The full-moon tell:** each month the gold ring in their eyes will show to anyone who looks, and Mara can't stay human to hide it.
- **The silver chain:** the tool of Cass's office literally burns Mara.

**What forces them together**
- **The Seven-Year Chain deadline:** the Highwood border lapses at first snow, so they have to finish the walk together on schedule, alone in the wilderness.
- **The compass sense:** once bonded, Cass can always find Mara, which makes her the only person who can track a Highwood wolf onto open ground and bring her back.
- **Shared pain:** if one is hurt, the other feels it, so they can't stay away while the other is in danger.
- **June's death:** someone killed the last surveyor on this route. Cass needs Mara's protection, and Mara needs Cass's honest survey to prove the Registrar is rigging the maps.

## Trilogy-level threads (for the outline)
- Who killed June, and why the Registrar is letting Highwood land lapse.
- Whether a human can be granted pack ground, and therefore whether Clause Nine can ever be rewritten.
- The Hollow's agenda: they could be allies against the treaty, or the threat that justifies it.
